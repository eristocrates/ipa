Building a Common Language 
Linguistic communication is harmonic. The goal is for the receiver
to understand the same meaning as the transmitter. Building a language is a
harmonic goal that strives for the same symbols to have the same meaning
for each member of the linguistic community that uses the language. 
Communication is about harmonizing -- the matching of the message to
the meaning. We each fit a message into our understanding. But we test the fit
by action and response, the feedback process.

 A child’s first word is usually “dada” or “mama,” a word that makes
the fundamental phonemic distinction between a consonant and a vowel.
Contrary to popular opinion, a phoneme is not a sound, but a set of
distinctions between sounds. 
The growing fluency of the child is a growing ability to distinguish between
more subtle differences between sounds and to reproduce mimetically the
same distinctions of sound. And eventually the individual learns the 
distinctions between the 20,000 to 35,000 words in the average vocabulary.

The Road to Agreement
Appreciative Process  - common models
The first step is usually the appreciative sharing of models. Often the
disagreements people have come from different models of the world. When
people listen to each other then they can see things from a different point of
view.  It creates a common model of the world that contains both points of
view.
We are taught in the popular media that the road to agreement is through
force. It generally involves beating people up or killing them. There is
another way.
The Influence Process – building common values
The sharing of different values generates a common values space. It
expands the range of considerations that the group has to recognize if a
common value is to be reached.
The Control Process – building common actions
Once there is a shared model and a common set of values, then the
alternatives available can be assessed in a way that recognizes the needs of
each member of the community.

Systemic Racism
When is racism a function of individuals and when is racism a function of “the
system.”  Is it authorized behaviour? 
Is race part of the corporate model of the world? 
It is important that there be a recognition of race.  This is good racism.
Race is a major factor in the modern world. It needs to be seen. 
What is the place of race in the corporate value set? 
Are racial differences valued? Is there racial preference? Is it
appropriate? Attempts to achieve a racial balance can be appropriate. The
problem is that the culture is historically racist. 
Is the corporate technology racist? 
Application forms. Job descriptions. Evaluation. Supervision. Promotion.
Customer service. Racial profiling and red-lining are notorious examples. 
Are the results racist? 
There is no question that the results of the justice system are racist. It is
an indicator that there is a racial bias in the processes. Even is this is a cultural bias
it can still be located and dealt with.
